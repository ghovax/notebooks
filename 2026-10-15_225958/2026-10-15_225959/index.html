Abstract

We turn indigo into epindolidione by exchanging two carbon–carbon bonds while every atom stays in the molecular plane: relaxed PM6-D3 scans from both molecules meet at the same barrier, the planar saddle is refined and its curvature examined, and the reaction path is followed down to both molecules, whose B3LYP electrostatic potentials are compared.

Keywords

indigo, epindolidione, carbon exchange, PM6-D3, transition state, IRC, electrostatic potential

WarningCaveats

The planar saddle has additional out-of-plane negative curvature, so it is not a full-space first-order TS.

The saddle has three imaginary modes, two of which leave the molecular plane. The 609.5\,\mathrm{kJ}\,\mathrm{mol}^{-1} electronic barrier therefore describes the constrained planar surface. The IRC termini have not been independently optimized and frequency-verified, so the pathway cannot establish an unconstrained first-order transition state.

Molecules and exchange coordinate

Indigo and epindolidione share one carbon framework: breaking the \ce{C2-C3} and \ce{C4-C5} bonds and forming \ce{C2-C4} and \ce{C3-C5} turns one into the other (Figure 1). PM6-D3 follows this exchange with every atom held in the molecular plane, inversion pairing and orientation retained, along the signed sum of the four carbon distances:

q=r_{2,3}+r_{4,5}-r_{2,4}-r_{3,5}.

The tables and discussion use the PM6-D3 electronic energy difference E-E_0, where E_0 is the relaxed indigo starting structure. The energy plots instead use the last recorded reverse IRC point as zero; it is not assumed to be an optimized minimum. The viewer follows the reaction path from indigo to epindolidione (Figure 2).

Energy along the exchange

Two relaxed scans, one starting from each molecule, fix q at 25 targets 0.1715\,\mathrm{\AA} apart; their maximum seeds the planar saddle search, and the IRC follows the in-plane path from the saddle down to both molecules (Figure 3).

The planar saddle lies 609.5\,\mathrm{kJ}\,\mathrm{mol}^{-1} above indigo, and epindolidione lies 96.3\,\mathrm{kJ}\,\mathrm{mol}^{-1} below it; the two scans give the same profile to within 0.004\,\mathrm{kJ}\,\mathrm{mol}^{-1} (Table 1).

Relaxed scans

Each target holds q fixed while the other in-plane coordinates relax, so the scan maximum is a constrained point, not a saddle (Figure 4). The two directions differ by \delta E=\Delta E_{\mathrm{indigo}}-\Delta E_{\mathrm{epindolidione}} at matched q (Figure 5). The RMS Cartesian gradient keeps the components along the constrained directions and need not vanish (Figure 6):

g_{\mathrm{RMS}}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{3N}\sum_{j=1}^{3N}\left(\frac{\partial E}{\partial x_j}\right)^2}.

Planar IRC

The IRC advances along the signed mass-weighted path length s, with the dashed line at s=0 marking the saddle and positive s toward epindolidione; every geometry stays planar:

ds^2=\sum_i m_i\,d\mathbf{R}_i\cdot d\mathbf{R}_i.

Its termini reach the indigo and epindolidione regions, within 0.1\,\mathrm{kJ}\,\mathrm{mol}^{-1} of the scan starting energies; they were not separately optimized (Figure 7, Figure 8).

Planar saddle and its three unstable modes

With q released and the planar restrictions kept, the saddle search converges to a structure with three imaginary modes. Only the strongest, k=1 at 1529.93i\,\mathrm{cm}^{-1}, lies in the plane and exchanges the carbon bonds; 274.76i and 25.06i\,\mathrm{cm}^{-1} move atoms out of plane, so this is a saddle within the plane, not a first-order saddle of the free molecule (Figure 9, Table 2). The out-of-plane share of each mode is:

f_z=\frac{\sum_i v_{ki,z}^2}{\sum_i\lVert\mathbf{v}_{ki}\rVert^2}.

Harmonic modes and IR spectrum

The animation displaces each atom i along the normalized eigenvector \mathbf v_{ki} with maximum excursion A=0.10\,\mathrm{\AA}; it shows the local direction of the mode, not a thermal amplitude:

\mathbf{R}_i(\phi)=\mathbf{R}_i^0+A\sin\phi\,\frac{\mathbf{v}_{ki}}{\max_j\lVert\mathbf{v}_{kj}\rVert}.

Electrostatic potential along the planar path

B3LYP/6\text{-}31{+}\mathrm{G}(d) single points at the two IRC termini and the planar saddle give the electrostatic potential V on the density surface \rho=0.002\,\mathrm{e}\,a_0^{-3}, on one colour scale for all three (Table 4, Figure 12). The most negative surface potential deepens from -1.55\,\mathrm{V} at the indigo terminus to -1.69\,\mathrm{V} at the epindolidione terminus, and the saddle has the largest positive extreme, 2.64\,\mathrm{V}.

Caveats

  • The plane is enforced throughout: two of the saddle’s three imaginary modes leave it, so the path and its 609.5\,\mathrm{kJ}\,\mathrm{mol}^{-1} barrier describe the planar surface only. The displacement TS experiments search without this restriction.
  • The IRC termini were not optimized or checked by frequencies; the fields sit on those termini, not on verified minima.
  • All energies are PM6-D3 electronic energies, without zero-point or thermal corrections. The B3LYP fields describe the PM6-D3 geometries and use a 0.2\,a_0 grid, which does not converge the core density.
