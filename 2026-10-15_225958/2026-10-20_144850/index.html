Abstract

We start indigo from two out-of-plane arrangements of its halves, one twisted perpendicular and one shifted into parallel offset planes, and test whether an unconstrained PM6-D3 search settles on a transition state for the central-bond rearrangement.

Keywords

indigo, PM6-D3, transition-state search, vibrational analysis

Caveats

Neither displaced guess reached a converged first-order saddle.

The perpendicular starting arrangement has 84 positive modes, the lowest at 27.1899\,\mathrm{cm}^{-1}, while the parallel-offset arrangement has negative curvature. Both retain residual forces under the standard convergence criteria: counting imaginary modes at these non-stationary geometries does not verify a minimum or transition state. No IRC was submitted from these rejected TS candidates.

Starting geometries

Both searches start from indigo with its halves pushed out of the molecular plane around the central C3–C4 bond, and let PM6-D3 move every atom freely toward a transition state (Figure 1):

  • Perpendicular halves: one half is rotated about C3–C4 until the \mathrm{N}_{13}–\mathrm{C}_{4}–\mathrm{C}_{3}–\mathrm{N}_{14} dihedral is 90^\circ.
  • Parallel offset halves: opposite rigid translations put the two halves in offset parallel planes, turning C3–C4 perpendicular to the original plane at unchanged length.

The frames follow successive optimizer evaluations, not physical time; the distances are the central bond and the four carbon contacts that would exchange.

Optimization outcome

Both searches use a calculated initial Hessian with updates every ten steps and accept the geometry only under loose custom thresholds; the energies follow the optimizer, relative to each starting geometry, and are not a barrier (Figure 3).

Neither search meets Gaussian’s standard convergence: both final geometries pass only the custom thresholds, which are 40 times looser, so substantial forces remain (Table 1).

Forces and optimizer records

The largest Cartesian force and the RMS gradient stay well above the standard thresholds throughout (Figure 4, Figure 5):

g_{\mathrm{RMS}}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{3N}\sum_{j=1}^{3N}\left(\frac{\partial E}{\partial x_j}\right)^2}.

Starting, highest-energy, largest-force, lowest-gradient and final evaluations of each search (Table 2, Table 3).

Curvature at the accepted geometries

Harmonic analysis at each accepted geometry, unscaled PM6-D3, tests for the single imaginary mode of a first-order saddle; both geometries share one wavenumber axis (Figure 6, Figure 7). The perpendicular start ends with all 84 modes real, lowest 27.19\,\mathrm{cm^{-1}}, and the parallel start with three imaginary modes, 86.50i, 79.54i and 55.08i\,\mathrm{cm^{-1}}: neither is a transition state, and no IRC follows (Table 4, Table 5).

Modes are numbered by increasing wavenumber in each geometry, so the difference pairs them by rank, not by motion: \Delta\widetilde{\nu}_k=\widetilde{\nu}_k^{\mathrm{parallel}}-\widetilde{\nu}_k^{\mathrm{perpendicular}} (Figure 8).

Lowest modes and real-mode spectrum

Mode k=1 of each geometry, displaced along the normalized eigenvector \mathbf v_{ki} with maximum excursion A=0.10\,\mathrm{\mathring{A}}; with forces remaining, these are local curvature directions, not vibrations of a stationary structure (Figure 9, Figure 10):

\mathbf{R}_i(\phi)=\mathbf{R}_i^0+A\sin\phi\,\frac{\mathbf{v}_{ki}}{\max_j\lVert\mathbf{v}_{kj}\rVert}.

Caveats

  • The custom thresholds accept geometries with large residual forces, so the harmonic analyses describe non-stationary structures.
  • Two starting geometries do not sample the out-of-plane surface; a converged search from other starts could still find a transition state.
  • All energies are PM6-D3 electronic energies, without zero-point or thermal corrections. The planar exchange keeps the plane and is a separate reference.
