Abstract

We predict which wavelengths the methoxy chelate absorbs from its ground state (S_0) and how strongly, then let the first excited state (S_1) relax to see how its shape and emission gap change, and compare with the measured fluorescence.

Molecule and ground-state geometry

Structure 1 of Saito et al. (2023), a methoxy difluoroboron chelate (\mathrm{X}=\mathrm{MeO}, \mathrm{Y}=\ce{B(OH)2}, \mathrm{Z}=\mathrm{H}), in IEFPCM acetonitrile with 6-31G(d,p). \mathrm{R} and \mathrm{R}' are the methoxyphenyl and phenylene rings (Figure 1). Its absorption and its relaxation in S_1 are computed with B3LYP and with DD-DFT, each starting from its own verified S_0 minimum \mathbf R_0.

Each labelled arrow is one Gaussian calculation, run with both methods; the curves are schematic and the values computed (Figure 2). The calculations stop at the S_1 minimum \mathbf R_1.

Molecular screening \varepsilon_s

Intrinsic molecular \varepsilon_s from each SCF density, not the solvent’s permittivity (Table 1, Figure 3).

  • DD-DFT converges to \varepsilon_s=2.4073, so \alpha=0.4154, against 2.7886 from the B3LYP density; at its own geometry DD-DFT gives \alpha=0.4185, which was not applied.
  • No measured permittivity or refractive index of 1 has been found, so \varepsilon_s has no experimental reference.

Absorption from the S_0 minimum

Measured molar absorption coefficient \epsilon\,[\mathrm{L\,mol^{-1}\,cm^{-1}}] in acetonitrile (Saito et al. 2023, SI Fig. S10A), with each calculated vertical transition drawn at its wavelength \lambda_n and with height \propto f_n (Figure 4). In both methods S_1 is a bright HOMO \rightarrow LUMO transition. DD-DFT sets its exact-exchange fraction from the molecule’s own screening, \alpha=1/\varepsilon_s=0.4154, about twice the 0.20 of B3LYP.

B3LYP places S_1 about 0.11\,\mathrm{eV} above the measured band maximum and DD-DFT about 0.50\,\mathrm{eV} above it; the published B3LYP value lies 0.03\,\mathrm{eV} below ours (Table 2).

Singlet roots, screening and transition character

The data behind the spectrum above, from the DD-DFT functional and the convergence of its screening to all ten singlet roots and the character of the bright S_1.

Excitation energies and the DD-DFT functional
  • Vertical excitation energy, with h Planck’s constant and c the speed of light: \Delta E_{S_n}=E_{S_n}(\mathbf R_0)-E_{S_0}(\mathbf R_0)=\frac{hc}{\lambda_n}.
  • DD-DFT sets the HF-exchange fraction from the molecular screening, \alpha=1/\varepsilon_s, weighting exchange and correlation by \alpha, 1-\alpha and \sqrt{1-\alpha}: E_{\mathrm{XC}}=0.4154\,E_{\mathrm{X}}^{\mathrm{HF}}+0.5846\,E_{\mathrm{X}}^{\mathrm{B88}}+0.7646\,E_{\mathrm{C}}^{\mathrm{LYP}}.
Convergence of the screening

\varepsilon_s at each SCF iteration n_{\mathrm{SCF}} and its distance from the final value (Figure 5, Figure 6). Table 3 lists the density-contour volume V_\rho, the orbital gap \Delta\epsilon (not optical), and the final density change and commutator.

All ten singlet roots

\Delta E_{S_n} of the ten lowest singlets S_n; DD-DFT raises every root (Figure 7).

Wavelength \lambda_n=hc/\Delta E_{S_n} and length-form oscillator strength f_n (Table 4, Table 5).

Character of the bright S_1 transition

The TD coefficient c of HOMO (89) \rightarrow LUMO (90) gives weight 2c^2=0.991 with B3LYP and 0.932 with DD-DFT, and one NTO pair carries 99.1\% and 97.7\% of the particle weight.

Particle-side NTO pairs k with weight w_k and share 100\eta_k, \eta_k=w_k/\sum_jw_j (Figure 8).

Length- and velocity-form strengths f_n^{(L)} and f_n^{(V)} agree for exact states; for S_1 they differ by about 7\% (Figure 9).

Construction of the fitted and calculated bands

The measured band over 360–520\,\mathrm{nm} is fitted with a Gaussian of amplitude A, center E_c and 1/e half-width w_{\mathrm{fit}} in photon energy E=hc/\lambda:

\epsilon_{\mathrm{fit}}(E)=A\exp\!\left[-\left(\frac{E-E_c}{w_{\mathrm{fit}}}\right)^2\right].

Fitted half-width: w_{\mathrm{fit}}=0.21547\,\mathrm{eV}. The center is an empirical envelope parameter.

Each f_n becomes a Gaussian band (Gaussian, Inc., n.d.) of the same width w in \mathrm{cm}^{-1}, with wavenumbers \widetilde{\nu}=1/\lambda and \widetilde{\nu}_n=1/\lambda_n; this sets the right-hand scale of Figure 4:

\epsilon(\widetilde{\nu})=\sum_n1.3062974\times10^8\frac{f_n}{w}\exp\!\left[-\left(\frac{\widetilde{\nu}-\widetilde{\nu}_n}{w}\right)^2\right].

Relaxation in S_1

Each S_1 optimization starts at its method’s \mathbf R_0 and ends at the relaxed S_1 geometry \mathbf R_1, with the solvent equilibrated to S_1; the conjugated core flattens and the chelate–phenylene twist falls to about 3^\circ. The S_1–S_0 gap at \mathbf R_1 is read from the last optimization step. It is compared with the measured fluorescence maximum, but no emission was calculated (Table 6): the B3LYP gap lies within 3\,\mathrm{nm} of the measured maximum, while DD-DFT is about 45\,\mathrm{nm} short of it.

Gaps, reorganization energies and geometry changes

S_1 gaps \Delta E_{S_1}(\mathbf R)=hc/\lambda_1 with the solvent equilibrated to S_1; \Delta E_{S_1}(\mathbf R_0) lies below the non-equilibrium value of Table 4, and the difference between the two gaps is the sum of the two reorganization energies (Table 7):

The gap narrows by the reorganization energies of the two states:

\Delta E_{S_1}(\mathbf R_0)-\Delta E_{S_1}(\mathbf R_1)=\underbrace{E_{S_1}(\mathbf R_0)-E_{S_1}(\mathbf R_1)}_{S_1\ \text{reorganization}}+\underbrace{E_{S_0}(\mathbf R_1)-E_{S_0}(\mathbf R_0)}_{S_0\ \text{reorganization}}.

Planarization of the conjugated core

Chelate–phenylene twist \theta and core planarity \delta; \delta falls to about 0.08\,\mathrm{\AA} in both methods (Table 8).

The twist \theta=\arccos\lvert\hat{\mathbf n}_1\cdot\hat{\mathbf n}_2\rvert is the angle between the least-squares planes of the chelate ring (with its boron) and the phenylene ring, with unit normals \hat{\mathbf n}_1 and \hat{\mathbf n}_2. The planarity \delta is the RMS distance of the M core heavy atoms (both aryl rings, the chelate ring and its boron) from their common least-squares plane:

\delta=\sqrt{\frac{1}{M}\sum_{k=1}^{M}\left[\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\left(\mathbf r_k-\bar{\mathbf r}\right)\right]^2}.

Frame 1 is \mathbf R_0 and frame 2 \mathbf R_1; each viewer labels the six bonds that change most (Figure 10, Figure 12), and the profile below each follows the conjugation path (Figure 11, Figure 13). The path is the shortest bonded route from the methyl carbon to the boronic boron, through both rings and the chelate backbone; each bond k sits at its midpoint distance s_k along the \mathbf R_0 path, with change

\Delta d_k=d_k(\mathbf R_1)-d_k(\mathbf R_0).

  • In S_1 the alternation inverts across the chelate–phenylene link: that bond shortens by about 0.045\,\mathrm{\AA} while its two neighbours lengthen by 0.02–0.04\,\mathrm{\AA}; the methoxyphenyl end and the boronic acid change by under 0.025\,\mathrm{\AA}.

Caveats

  • A band maximum is not a vertical energy, and the measured trace’s top points (399–400\,\mathrm{nm}) touch the figure frame.
  • Their basis is 6-31+G(d), ours 6-31G(d,p).
  • Only absorption at \mathbf R_0 and relaxation in S_1 were calculated; emission at \mathbf R_1 (TD with non-equilibrium solvation) and the return to \mathbf R_0 on S_0 were not. The gap at \mathbf R_1 is only compared with the fluorescence maximum, and vibronic structure is not included.
