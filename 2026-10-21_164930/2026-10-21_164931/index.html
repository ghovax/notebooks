Abstract

We compute the electronic screening of benzene, then use it to predict which wavelengths benzene absorbs from its ground state (S_0) and how strongly.

Keywords

benzene, DD-DFT, vertical excitations, absorption

Molecule and ground-state geometry

Isolated benzene with the Cartesian 6-31G(d,p) basis; the viewer shows the B3LYP S_0 minimum \mathbf R_0 (Figure 1). Molecular screening \varepsilon_s and vertical S_0\rightarrow S_n absorption are computed with B3LYP and with DD-DFT, each at its own frequency-verified \mathbf R_0, and compared with the measured gas-phase spectrum.

Each labelled arrow is one Gaussian calculation, run with both methods; the curves are schematic and the values computed (Figure 2). The calculations stop at the vertical excitations from \mathbf R_0.

Molecular screening \varepsilon_s

Intrinsic molecular \varepsilon_s from each SCF density, with the measured static permittivity \varepsilon and squared refractive index n_D^2 of liquid benzene (Maryott and Smith 1951; Moutzouris et al. 2014) (Table 1, Figure 3).

  • DD-DFT converges to \varepsilon_s=2.16872, so \alpha=0.4611, against 2.44296 from the B3LYP density; at its own geometry DD-DFT gives 2.16310, 0.26\% lower, which was not applied.
  • Liquid benzene has \varepsilon=2.274 at 25\,^\circ\mathrm C: DD-DFT lies 4.6\% below it and B3LYP 7.4\% above. The electronic part alone, n_D^2=2.237, is closer still to DD-DFT.

Absorption from the S_0 minimum

Measured molar absorption coefficient \epsilon\,[\mathrm{L\,mol^{-1}\,cm^{-1}}] of benzene vapour at 295\,\mathrm K (Capalbo et al. 2016; Keller-Rudek et al. 2013) with the calculated transitions at \lambda_n, height \propto f_n (Figure 4).

Both methods give a dark S_1 and a degenerate bright pair, S_3 and S_4. DD-DFT sets its exact-exchange fraction from the molecule’s own screening, \alpha=1/\varepsilon_s=0.4611, against 0.20 in B3LYP, and raises every root. It places the bright pair 0.2103\,\mathrm{eV} above B3LYP, and both lie at shorter wavelength than the measured trace, which starts at 171.47\,\mathrm{nm}, so these data cannot rank the methods (Table 2).

Singlet roots, screening and transition character

The data behind the spectrum above, from the DD-DFT functional and the convergence of its screening to all ten singlet roots and the character of the lowest transitions.

Excitation energies and the DD-DFT functional
  • Vertical excitation energy, with h Planck’s constant and c the speed of light: \Delta E_{S_n}=E_{S_n}(\mathbf R_0)-E_{S_0}(\mathbf R_0)=\frac{hc}{\lambda_n}.
  • DD-DFT sets the HF-exchange fraction from the molecular screening, \alpha=1/\varepsilon_s, weighting exchange and correlation by \alpha, 1-\alpha and \sqrt{1-\alpha}: E_{\mathrm{XC}}=0.4611\,E_{\mathrm{X}}^{\mathrm{HF}}+0.5389\,E_{\mathrm{X}}^{\mathrm{B88}}+0.7341\,E_{\mathrm{C}}^{\mathrm{LYP}}.
Convergence of the screening

\varepsilon_s at each SCF iteration n_{\mathrm{SCF}}, with n_{\mathrm{SCF}}=0 the initial value, and its distance from the final value (Figure 5, Figure 6). Table 3 lists the density-contour volume V_\rho, the orbital gap \Delta\epsilon (not optical), and the final density change and commutator.

All ten singlet roots

\Delta E_{S_n} of the ten lowest singlets S_n (Figure 7).

Wavelength \lambda_n=hc/\Delta E_{S_n} and length-form oscillator strength f_n (Table 4, Table 5).

Character of the lowest transitions
  • The dark S_1 splits into two equal NTO pairs, each carrying 49.9\% of the particle weight in both methods, so no single orbital pair describes it.
  • The bright pair’s length- and velocity-form strengths agree within 1\%.

Particle-side NTO pairs k of the S_0\rightarrow S_1 transition with weight w_k and share 100\eta_k, \eta_k=w_k/\sum_jw_j (Figure 8).

Length- and velocity-form strengths f_n^{(L)} and f_n^{(V)} agree for exact states; weak higher transitions differ more (Figure 9).

Construction of the fitted and calculated bands

The measured cross section \sigma_{\mathrm{abs}}\,[\mathrm{cm}^2] per molecule converts to \epsilon=N_{\mathrm A}\sigma_{\mathrm{abs}}/(1000\ln10), with N_{\mathrm A} Avogadro’s constant. The measured band is fitted with a Gaussian of amplitude A, center E_c and 1/e half-width w_{\mathrm{fit}} in photon energy E=hc/\lambda:

\epsilon_{\mathrm{fit}}(E)=A\exp\!\left[-\left(\frac{E-E_c}{w_{\mathrm{fit}}}\right)^2\right].

Fitted half-width: w_{\mathrm{fit}}=0.18865\,\mathrm{eV}. The center is an empirical envelope parameter.

Each f_n becomes a Gaussian band (Gaussian, Inc., n.d.) of the same width w in \mathrm{cm}^{-1}, with wavenumbers \widetilde{\nu}=1/\lambda and \widetilde{\nu}_n=1/\lambda_n; this sets the right-hand scale of Figure 4:

\epsilon(\widetilde{\nu})=\sum_n1.3062974\times10^8\frac{f_n}{w}\exp\!\left[-\left(\frac{\widetilde{\nu}-\widetilde{\nu}_n}{w}\right)^2\right].

Caveats

  • A band maximum is not a vertical energy; ranking the methods needs a vibronic band assignment.
  • S_1 and S_2 have zero calculated f_n; vibronic intensity is not modelled.
  • The measured \varepsilon and n_D^2 belong to the liquid, where neighbouring molecules also screen; \varepsilon_s is that of one molecule.
  • The basis lacks diffuse functions. Only vertical absorption at \mathbf R_0 was calculated: no excited state was optimized, no emission was calculated, and the spectral effect of the rechecked DD-DFT weights is not included.
