Screening uses molecular polarizability and volume, not bulk-liquid permittivity.

  • i\in\mathcal O and a\in\mathcal V index occupied and virtual orbitals.
  • The volume V encloses density above 0.001\,a_0^{-3}.

For a doubly occupied reference, the isotropic response averages three Cartesian components:

\bar{\alpha}_{\mathrm{unc}}=\frac{1}{3}\operatorname{tr}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\mathrm{unc}} =\frac{4}{3}\sum_{i\in\mathcal O}\sum_{a\in\mathcal V} \frac{\left\lVert\langle\phi_i|\mathbf r|\phi_a\rangle\right\rVert^2}{\epsilon_a-\epsilon_i}

The squared norm sums Cartesian components. Screening divides polarizability by molecular volume:

\varepsilon_s=1+\frac{4\pi}{V}\,\bar{\alpha}_{\mathrm{unc}}

Self-consistent screening repeats the SCF until the applied and evaluated \varepsilon_s agree:

E_{\mathrm{XC}}=\alpha\,E_{\mathrm{X}}^{\mathrm{HF}}+(1-\alpha)\,E_{\mathrm{X}}^{\mathrm{B88}}+\sqrt{1-\alpha}\,E_{\mathrm{C}}^{\mathrm{LYP}},\qquad \alpha=\frac{1}{\varepsilon_s}
