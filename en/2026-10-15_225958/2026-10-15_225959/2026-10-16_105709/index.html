WarningCaveats

The planar stationary point has the intended in-plane negative mode, but two additional out-of-plane imaginary modes mean it is not a full-space first-order saddle.

Kind Message
Assumption of classical behavior for rotation may cause significant error.
Explicit consideration of 27 degrees of freedom as vibrations may cause significant error.
Gaussian found 3 imaginary modes; 1 was expected.
The saddle was identified under constraints; this alone does not establish a first-order saddle in the full coordinate space.

System

The system the job treated, its symmetry and its electrons; the method and basis are those of the input above.

  • N_\alpha and N_\beta count spin-up and spin-down electrons; together they are the nuclear charges Z_A less the charge q.
  • Their difference fixes the spin projection M_S=(N_\alpha-N_\beta)/2; the usual highest-weight input has S=M_S.
  • Closed shells have doubly occupied orbitals; equal spin counts alone do not establish one.

N_\alpha+N_\beta=\sum_A Z_A-q,\qquad 2S+1=N_\alpha-N_\beta+1

Output

SCF convergence

The job recorded 8 self-consistent field solutions, one per geometry or state it evaluated. The chart shows the number of cycles each SCF needed, against its index.

Geometry optimization

Gaussian printed 8 convergence checks, in output order, against their limits:

  • Each energy E_n is measured from that of the last check printed, E_n-E_N, which is not assumed converged.
  • The forces are given by their largest value, F_{\max}=\max_i\lvert F_i\rvert, and their root mean square, F_{\mathrm{RMS}}=\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}F_i^2}, over the N coordinates the optimization uses.
  • Displacements \Delta q are in bohr for distances and radians for angles.

Geometries along the job

Printed geometries in order; faded frames show the preceding structures.

Vibrational frequencies

The latest analysis has 84 modes, 3 of them imaginary, shown with their IR intensities over approximate mid-infrared regions, unscaled:

  • Each wavenumber follows from the mode’s force constant and reduced mass, \tilde{\nu}_k=\frac{1}{2\pi c}\sqrt{k_k/\mu_k}.
  • The IR intensity grows with the squared change of the dipole along the mode, I_k\propto\lvert\partial\boldsymbol{\mu}/\partial Q_k\rvert^2.
  • A mode is imaginary when k_k<0, a direction of negative curvature.

Normal-mode frequencies

Negative wavenumbers mark imaginary modes, not absorption frequencies.

Normal-mode animations

The imaginary modes, the six lowest real modes and the four most IR-intense ones, each scaled so that its largest atomic displacement is

\max_A\lVert\mathbf{u}_{kA}\rVert=0.6\,\text{\AA},

with \mathbf{u}_{kA} the displacement of atom A in mode k.

Frontier orbitals

The highest occupied and lowest unoccupied levels, and their gap, which is not an optical excitation energy:

\Delta\epsilon=\epsilon_{\mathrm{LUMO}}-\epsilon_{\mathrm{HOMO}}

Levels near the gap, one column per symmetry:

  • Hover a level to name it; click it to highlight it.
  • Labels use C_{2h} symmetry, with lowercase for orbitals and capitals for states.

The orbitals in space, chosen in the dropdowns:

  • Red surfaces enclose \psi<-\psi_0, blue ones \psi>+\psi_0, at the level \psi_0 whose surfaces hold 85\,\% of the orbital’s density \int\psi^2\,\mathrm{d}V; the settings show and move it.

Atomic charges and electric moments

Mulliken charges for the final evaluated geometry, when a matching SCF population analysis was printed:

  • Red atoms carry q_A<0, blue ones q_A>0 and white ones q_A\approx0, on a scale symmetric about zero.
  • Mulliken populations split shared density equally, \mathbf P being the density and \mathbf S the overlap matrix, so compare charges only within one method and basis.

q_A=Z_A-\sum_{\mu\in A}(\mathbf{P}\mathbf{S})_{\mu\mu}

The dipole moment of the same density, in its printed orientation:

\boldsymbol{\mu}=\begin{pmatrix}0 \\ 0 \\ 0\end{pmatrix}\,\mathrm{D}=\begin{pmatrix}0 \\ 0 \\ 0\end{pmatrix}\,e a_0,\qquad |\boldsymbol{\mu}|=0\,\mathrm{D}=0\,e a_0

Static polarizability

The last complete response gives the static polarizability tensor and its isotropic average, with the defining energy derivative:

\boldsymbol{\alpha}=-\frac{\partial^2E}{\partial F_i\,\partial F_j}=\begin{pmatrix}602.714 & -131.637 & \mathord{5.74766\times 10^{-10}} \\ -131.637 & 290.312 & \mathord{-8.26787\times 10^{-10}} \\ \mathord{5.74766\times 10^{-10}} & \mathord{-8.26787\times 10^{-10}} & 31.2138\end{pmatrix}\,a_0^3,\qquad \bar{\alpha}=\tfrac{1}{3}\operatorname{tr}\boldsymbol{\alpha}=308.08\,a_0^3
