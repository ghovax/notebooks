Electronic structure and screening

Screening uses molecular polarizability and volume, not bulk-liquid permittivity.

  • i\in\mathcal O and a\in\mathcal V index occupied and virtual orbitals.
  • The volume V encloses density above 0.001\,a_0^{-3}.

For a doubly occupied reference, the isotropic part of the uncoupled (zeroth-order) polarizability \boldsymbol{\alpha}^{(0)} averages three Cartesian components:

\bar{\alpha}^{(0)}=\frac{1}{3}\operatorname{tr}\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{(0)} =\frac{4}{3}\sum_{i\in\mathcal O}\sum_{a\in\mathcal V} \frac{\left\lVert\langle\phi_i|\mathbf r|\phi_a\rangle\right\rVert^2}{\epsilon_a-\epsilon_i}

The squared norm sums Cartesian components. Screening divides polarizability by molecular volume:

\varepsilon_s=1+\frac{4\pi}{V}\,\bar{\alpha}^{(0)}

Self-consistent screening repeats the SCF until the applied and evaluated \varepsilon_s agree:

E_{\mathrm{XC}}=\alpha\,E_{\mathrm{X}}^{\mathrm{HF}}+(1-\alpha)\,E_{\mathrm{X}}^{\mathrm{B88}}+\sqrt{1-\alpha}\,E_{\mathrm{C}}^{\mathrm{LYP}},\qquad \alpha=\frac{1}{\varepsilon_s}

Energy components

Coulomb, weighted exact exchange, and density-functional exchange–correlation contributions.

These components omit kinetic, electron–nuclear and nuclear-repulsion terms; density-functional exchange and correlation remain combined.
