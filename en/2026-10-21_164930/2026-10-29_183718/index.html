Comparison with the checkpoint-chained workflow

Rerunning benzene without checkpoint reuse reproduces the checkpoint-chained results. In Table 1 and Table 2:

  • The B3LYP total energies differ by 0.03\,\mathrm{meV}.
  • The DD-DFT total energies differ by 12\,\mathrm{meV}, because the two optimizations stop at slightly different geometries.
  • The gaps \Delta\epsilon are about 6.79\,\mathrm{eV} for B3LYP and 8.86\,\mathrm{eV} for DD-DFT in both workflows.

In Table 3 and Table 4, the shift \delta\Delta E_{S_n} is \Delta E_{S_n} with independent guesses minus \Delta E_{S_n} with chained guesses:

  • Both workflows give a dark S_1 and a degenerate bright S_3/S_4 pair.
  • Every \delta\Delta E_{S_n} is at most a few milli-electronvolts.

In Table 5, the shift \delta\varepsilon_s/\varepsilon_s is \varepsilon_s with independent guesses divided by \varepsilon_s with chained guesses, minus one:

  • The DD-DFT screening \varepsilon_s changes by -0.10\,\mathrm{\%} at the B3LYP geometry.
  • It changes by -0.08\,\mathrm{\%} at the DD-DFT geometry.

Orbital eigenvalues printed after the natural transition orbitals

The checkpoint-chained excitation logs show a “no gap” pattern that is not an orbital gap:

  • Their last job requests natural transition orbitals (Guess=Only Density=(Check,Transition=1) Pop=SaveNTO).
  • Gaussian prints its weights under the headings Alpha occ. eigenvalues and Alpha virt. eigenvalues.
  • These are transition-density eigenvalues, not orbital energies.
  • The occupied block ends at 0.49968 for B3LYP and 0.50073 for DD-DFT, and the virtual block starts at the same values.
  • The SCF orbital energies printed earlier keep the gaps of Table 1 and Table 2.
  • The independent-guess logs request no natural transition orbitals, so they carry no such block.
