Abstract

Transition-state refinement, IRC following and endpoint optimizations for the parent Diels–Alder reaction of 1,3-butadiene and ethene, from a guess with the fragments in parallel offset planes.

  • The saddle refinement converged to a first-order saddle with one imaginary mode, \tilde\nu=530.1194i\,\mathrm{cm}^{-1}, forming C–C bonds of length r=2.2675\,\mathrm{\AA} and a stable closed-shell wavefunction.

Frontier orbitals

The orbitals are recalculated in PySCF at B3LYP/6-31G(d,p) in Gaussian’s form, B3LYPG, whose orbital energies differ from the saddle run’s by at most \max_i\lvert\Delta\epsilon_i\rvert=0.3\,\mathrm{meV}.

  • The fragments \ce{C4H6} and \ce{C2H4} are calculated alone at each geometry, without polarization by the partner.
  • The complex \ce{C6H10} is the whole system at the same geometry, calculated as one molecule that holds both fragments.
  • The symmetry labels are those of the complex’s point group C_{s}, found from the geometry at every point of the path.

Orbital interactions at the saddle

Each orbital \psi_i of the complex mixes the fragment orbitals \chi_k, with Mulliken weights w_{ik} that add up to one:

\psi_i=\sum_k x_{ik}\chi_k,\qquad w_{ik}=x_{ik}\sum_l x_{il}\langle\chi_k|\chi_l\rangle \tag{1}

Each occupied frontier orbital of the complex mixes mainly one frontier pair:

\psi_{\mathrm{HOMO}_{1}}\approx c_1\,\psi_\mathrm{HOMO}(\ce{C4H6})+c_2\,\psi_\mathrm{LUMO}(\ce{C2H4}),\qquad \psi_{\mathrm{HOMO}}\approx c_3\,\psi_\mathrm{HOMO}(\ce{C2H4})+c_4\,\psi_\mathrm{LUMO}(\ce{C4H6})

  • The first is normal electron demand, all three orbitals a''; the second inverse, all a'.
  • One symmetry per pair is the Woodward–Hoffmann condition for a thermal [4+2] cycloaddition.

Atom pairs

Each overlap splits into atom pairs (A,B), one atom of each fragment, c_\mu and d_\nu being the donor and acceptor coefficients; the plot sums them by group G, and S' alike:

S=\sum_{A\in\ce{C4H6}}\sum_{B\in\ce{C2H4}}S_{A,B},\qquad S_{A,B}=\sum_{\mu\in A}\sum_{\nu\in B}c_\mu d_\nu\langle\phi_\mu|\phi_\nu\rangle,\qquad S_G=\sum_{(A,B)\in G}S_{A,B} \tag{2}

  • Primary carbons form the bonds \ce{C1-C6} and \ce{C4-C5}.
  • Terminal carbons pair reacting atoms that do not bond.
  • Secondary carbons involve \ce{C2} or \ce{C3}, which do not react.
  • Hydrogens involve a hydrogen that does not react.
  • The forming pairs give S_G=0.321 and S'_G=0.279, more than either whole overlap, because the other pairs subtract from it.

The viewer shows a pair’s product kept to one group G of atom pairs, whose integral is that group’s share in Figure 2:

f_G=\sum_{(A,B)\in G}\psi^{(A)}\psi^{(B)},\qquad \psi^{(A)}=\sum_{\mu\in A}c_\mu\phi_\mu

  • \psi^{(A)} is the part of the donor’s \mathrm{HOMO} on atom A, and \psi^{(B)} that of the acceptor’s \mathrm{LUMO} on atom B.
  • Each surface encloses a share \eta=90\% of \int|f_G|\,\mathrm{d}V.

Along the reaction path

The frontier quantities follow the IRC’s geometries, every tenth point recalculated as at the saddle:

  • Negative s leads to cyclohexene, positive s to the separated fragments.
  • Each point has its own fragments and complex, so the orbitals follow the changing geometry.
  • Bond formation is synchronous, since the lengths r_{1,6} and r_{4,5} of the two forming bonds differ by at most \max_s\lvert r_{1,6}-r_{4,5}\rvert=0.0021\,\mathrm{\AA}.
  • The reaction is concerted, not stepwise, since the energy along s has one maximum, the saddle, and no intermediate.
  • Toward the separated fragments the \mathrm{HOMO} is mostly \psi_\mathrm{HOMO}(\ce{C4H6}), w_{ik}=99\,\mathrm{\%}, and the \mathrm{HOMO}_{1} mostly \psi_\mathrm{HOMO}(\ce{C2H4}), w_{ik}=95\,\mathrm{\%}.
  • Toward cyclohexene the \mathrm{HOMO} is the \ce{C2=C3} \pi bond, with w_{\ce{C2},p}+w_{\ce{C3},p}=83\,\mathrm{\%} on the p functions of \ce{C2} and \ce{C3}.
  • The reaction is symmetry-allowed without light, since both ends fill the same valence orbitals of each symmetry, 10 a', 7 a'', and no filled level must empty.

The Klopman–Salem equation turns both overlaps into one frontier stabilization, with k an unknown constant:

\Delta E\approx-2k^2F,\qquad F=\frac{S^2}{\Delta\epsilon}+\frac{S'^2}{\Delta\epsilon'} \tag{3}

\Delta\epsilon=\epsilon_\mathrm{LUMO}(\ce{C2H4})-\epsilon_\mathrm{HOMO}(\ce{C4H6}),\qquad \Delta\epsilon'=\epsilon_\mathrm{LUMO}(\ce{C4H6})-\epsilon_\mathrm{HOMO}(\ce{C2H4})

  • F leaves out the closed-shell repulsion and the distortion of the fragments.
  • The barrier comes from the terms F leaves out, since F grows steadily from the separated fragments to the ring, with no maximum at the saddle.
  • Both frontier pairs matter, since at the saddle S^2/(S^2+S'^2)=57\,\mathrm{\%}.

The weights of Equation 1 include the polarization that the fragments alone lack, and count the electrons each fragment’s \mathrm{LUMO} receives from the occupied orbitals \psi_i, the set \mathcal{O}:

n=2\sum_{i\in\mathcal{O}}w_{i,\mathrm{LUMO}}

At the saddle:

  • \psi_\mathrm{LUMO}(\ce{C2H4}) holds n=0.30 electrons, the donation of normal demand.
  • \psi_\mathrm{LUMO}(\ce{C4H6}) holds n=0.31 electrons, the back-donation of inverse demand.
  • The two are close, as expected without substituents.
