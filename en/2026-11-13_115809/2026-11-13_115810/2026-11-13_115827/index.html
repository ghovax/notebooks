System

The system the job treated, its symmetry and its electrons; the method and basis are those of the input above.

  • N_\alpha and N_\beta count spin-up and spin-down electrons; together they are the nuclear charges Z_A less the charge q.
  • Their difference fixes the spin projection M_S; the usual highest-weight input has S=M_S.
  • Closed shells have doubly occupied orbitals; equal spin counts alone do not establish one.

N_\alpha+N_\beta=\sum_A Z_A-q,\qquad M_S=\frac{N_\alpha-N_\beta}{2},\qquad 2S+1=N_\alpha-N_\beta+1

Input

Link 0 commands

Link 0 is the part of each job before its route: it sets this job’s memory, processors and files. Every linked job has its own.

Route keywords and options

The route selects the method, basis and job type.

Redundant internal coordinate changes

  • ModRedundant adds, freezes, scans or removes redundant internal coordinates before the optimization starts.
  • Gaussian may print a coordinate with its atoms reversed.

Internal options

IOp settings override defaults; meanings follow the IOps Reference.

Molecule specification

The job’s title, charge and spin multiplicity, and where Gaussian took its geometry from.

Starting geometry

The structure the calculation starts from. Bonds, angles and dihedrals the input’s coordinate section defines are measured on it.

Output

SCF convergence

The job recorded 41 self-consistent field solutions, one per geometry or state it evaluated. The chart shows the number of cycles each SCF needed, against its index.

Geometry optimization

Gaussian printed 40 convergence checks, in output order, against their limits:

  • Each energy E_n is measured from that of the last check printed, E_n-E_N, which is not assumed converged.
  • The forces are given by their largest value and their root mean square over the N coordinates the optimization uses: F_{\max}=\max_i\lvert F_i\rvert,\qquad F_{\mathrm{RMS}}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}F_i^2}
  • Displacements \Delta q are in bohr for distances and radians for angles.

Geometries along the job

Printed geometries in order; faded frames show the preceding structures.

Frontier orbitals

The highest occupied and lowest unoccupied levels, and their gap, which is not an optical excitation energy:

\Delta\epsilon=\epsilon_{\mathrm{LUMO}}-\epsilon_{\mathrm{HOMO}}

Levels near the gap, one column per symmetry:

  • Hover a level to name it; click it to highlight it.
  • The molecule has no symmetry, point group C_1, so orbitals are named from the frontier: \mathrm{HOMO}_{n} below the highest occupied and \mathrm{LUMO}_{n} above the lowest unoccupied.
WarningSurfaces not drawn

Excited states and spectrum

The job solved for 6 excited states. The spectrum places each state at its wavelength \lambda_n with its oscillator strength f_n, over conventional ultraviolet bands.

Oscillator strength measures the transition dipole moment \boldsymbol\mu_{0n} between the ground state and state n, the matrix element of the dipole operator \hat{\boldsymbol\mu}=-\sum_i\hat{\mathbf r}_i (atomic units):

f_n=\frac{2}{3}\,\Delta E_n\,\lvert\boldsymbol\mu_{0n}\rvert^2,\qquad \boldsymbol\mu_{0n}=\langle\Psi_0|\hat{\boldsymbol\mu}|\Psi_n\rangle=-\int\mathbf r\,\rho_{0n}(\mathbf r)\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf r

Without the symmetry of the states, only f_n tells bright states from dark ones.

Each bar stacks the weights of one state’s transitions between orbital sets:

  • A transition’s weight sums over its printed configurations; an unrestricted job counts c_{ia}^2 per spin instead of 2c_{ia}^2: w=\sum 2c_{ia}^2
  • Configurations with \lvert c_{ia}\rvert<0.1 are not printed, so \sum w\le 100\,\%.
  • Dark states, f<0.001, are faded.
  • The molecule has no symmetry, point group C_1, so orbitals are named from the frontier: \mathrm{HOMO}_{n} below the highest occupied and \mathrm{LUMO}_{n} above the lowest unoccupied.
