System

The system the job treated, its symmetry and its electrons; the method and basis are those of the input above.

  • N_\alpha and N_\beta count spin-up and spin-down electrons; together they are the nuclear charges Z_A less the charge q.
  • Their difference fixes the spin projection M_S=(N_\alpha-N_\beta)/2; the usual highest-weight input has S=M_S.
  • Closed shells have doubly occupied orbitals; equal spin counts alone do not establish one.

N_\alpha+N_\beta=\sum_A Z_A-q,\qquad 2S+1=N_\alpha-N_\beta+1

Input

Link 0 commands

Link 0 is the part of each job before its route: it sets this job’s memory, processors and files. Every linked job has its own.

Route keywords and options

The route selects the method, basis and job type.

Output

SCF convergence

The job recorded 1 self-consistent field solution. The chart shows the RMS density change by cycle; the dashed line is the threshold.

Frontier orbitals

The highest occupied and lowest unoccupied levels, and their gap, which is not an optical excitation energy:

\Delta\epsilon=\epsilon_{\mathrm{LUMO}}-\epsilon_{\mathrm{HOMO}}

Levels near the gap, one column per symmetry:

  • Hover a level to name it; click it to highlight it.
  • Labels use C_{s} symmetry, with lowercase for orbitals and capitals for states.
  • Gaussian computed in C_{1}, so the labels it printed belong to that group.
  • Surfaces enclose the phases \psi>+\psi_0 and \psi<-\psi_0, holding 85\,\% of \int\psi^2\,\mathrm{d}V; the overall sign is arbitrary.
  • Parts by angular momentum l or atom A, with Mulliken shares summing to 100\,\%: \psi^{(l)}=\sum_{\mu:\,l_\mu=l}c_\mu\phi_\mu,\qquad w_{A,l}=100\sum_{\mu\in A,\,l_\mu=l}c_\mu(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{c})_\mu

Electrostatic potential

The saved electrostatic potential V of the final density, in two views, switched by the last button of the viewer’s toolbar:

  • On the density surface \rho=0.002\,\mathrm{e}\,a_0^{-3}, red marks where a positive charge is attracted and blue where it is repelled, on one scale symmetric about zero that covers 95\,\% of the surface, so the few points of strongest potential do not wash the rest out.
  • In space, red surfaces enclose V<-V_0 and blue ones V>+V_0, V_0 being the median \lvert V\rvert over the density surface, so they bulge out where the potential is stronger than over most of the molecule; the settings lower V_0.

Atomic charges and electric moments

Mulliken charges for the final evaluated geometry, when a matching SCF population analysis was printed:

  • Red atoms carry q_A<0, blue ones q_A>0 and white ones q_A\approx0, on a scale symmetric about zero.
  • Mulliken populations split shared density equally, \mathbf P being the density and \mathbf S the overlap matrix, so compare charges only within one method and basis.

q_A=Z_A-\sum_{\mu\in A}(\mathbf{P}\mathbf{S})_{\mu\mu}

The dipole moment of the same density, in its printed orientation:

\boldsymbol{\mu}=\begin{pmatrix}-1.121 \\ 0.5042 \\ \mathord{6\times 10^{-4}}\end{pmatrix}\,\mathrm{D}=\begin{pmatrix}-0.441035 \\ 0.198368 \\ \mathord{2.36058\times 10^{-4}}\end{pmatrix}\,e a_0,\qquad |\boldsymbol{\mu}|=1.2292\,\mathrm{D}=0.483604\,e a_0

The traceless quadrupole moment of the same density, in atomic units and debye ångström:

\mathbf{Q}=\begin{pmatrix}12.4853 & 4.44287 & 0.0048872 \\ 4.44287 & -3.01647 & 0.0072377 \\ 0.0048872 & 0.0072377 & -9.46882\end{pmatrix}\,\mathrm{e}\,a_0^{2}=\begin{pmatrix}16.7931 & 5.97581 & 0.00657345 \\ 5.97581 & -4.05725 & 0.00973495 \\ 0.00657345 & 0.00973495 & -12.7359\end{pmatrix}\,\mathrm{D}\,\text{\AA}
